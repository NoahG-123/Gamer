Fifty years later, Colleen Mahar's daughter is still asking

By Anita Kowalczyk

SACKVILLE, N.B. — Maureen Mahar-Lyle keeps her mother in a shoebox. A green wool coat button. A photo-booth strip from the Halifax Shopping Centre, 1970, four frames of a dark-haired girl laughing at something off camera. A copy of a police report so faded it is nearly blank. And a letter, never sent, that Maureen wrote to her mother when she was fourteen.

"I was eight months old," she says. "I don't remember her. I remember my grandmother remembering her. That's what I have."

Colleen Mahar was 21 when she walked out of the Seahorse Tavern on Argyle Street in the early hours of October 9, 1971, got into a car with two men she knew, and was never seen again. Within a month, police had concluded she had likely left for Toronto. Her mother, Bernadette, refused to accept it until her death in 1998. Maureen, a retired teacher now 50, has refused to accept it since.

"Everybody who was in that car is old now. One of them is dead. I'd just like the other one to say what happened before he's dead too. I'm not even angry anymore. I just want to know where she is."

Halifax Regional Police confirmed the file is still open and classified as a historical missing person case. A spokesperson said there had been "no new investigative developments in several decades."

The two men who drove Colleen Mahar home that night were never charged and have always maintained they dropped her off at the corner of Barrington and Morris. One died in 2003. The other, now 79, is a well-known figure on the Halifax waterfront. He declined, through his son, to be interviewed for this story.

Maureen visits Halifax twice a year. She walks from Argyle Street to Barrington and Morris, which takes eleven minutes. Then she keeps walking, down to the water.

"Somebody knows," she says. "Somebody has always known."
